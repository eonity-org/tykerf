# Event

Something that happens on a date: a wedding, a concert, a workshop, a conference, a celebration. A visitor wants to know what it is, when and where, whether they can come, and how to say they will.

## What the page needs
- What it is, the date and the place, all in the first screen.
- The programme: times in order, what happens at each, who is on.
- The people, when they matter to the visitor (the band, the speakers, the couple).
- Where: the venue's name and address, a link to a map, how to get there and where to park or sleep, if the request says.
- How to come: tickets, registration or a reply (RSVP), with its deadline.
- The questions people ask (dress code, children, accessibility, refunds), when the request gives the answers.

## What only the person can tell you
The date and times, the venue, the prices, the ticket or registration service, the deadline, the names of the people. Use what the request gives. When one is missing, don't present a made-up value as real: write a plain example and list it at the end of your answer to confirm. A date is written with the weekday and the year, in the page's language.

## Links
- Tickets or registration on another service: its full address (https://…). Without the real one, the form on the page (contact) can take the replies; say so in your answer.
- A map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=<the address>.
- A calendar or a stream: only with the address the person gives.

## Watch for
- After the date, the page says it happened; mention in your answer that the person may want to change it then.
- A wedding page is personal and warm, a conference page plain and practical: the tone follows the event.

# Organisation

A group with a purpose: an association, a club, a school, a charity, a council. A visitor wants to know what it stands for, what it does, whether they belong there, and how to join, help or ask.

## What the page needs
- What it stands for, in one or two plain sentences, with a picture of its people or its work.
- What it does: activities, services or programmes, each with who it is for and when.
- Its people, when the visitor needs to know them (a head, a team, the volunteers), with a role each.
- A few figures, only true ones the request gives (members, years, people helped).
- How to take part: join, volunteer, donate, enrol, with what the step involves (a fee, a form, a date).
- How to reach it: an address and hours if people come, an email, a phone.

## What only the person can tell you
Names and roles, figures, fees, dates, the address, the bank or donation service, the registration number of a charity. Use what the request gives. Never make up a figure or a person: leave the section out, or write a plain example and list it at the end of your answer to confirm.

## Links
- A donation, a membership or an enrolment service: its full address (https://…); without the real one, link to the contact section and say so in your answer.
- Pages the site doesn't have yet (the statutes, the calendar, a privacy policy): at the address they will have (/calendar/), named in your answer.
- Social profiles: their full address, only with the real one.

## Watch for
- A school or a council speaks to several groups (parents, pupils, neighbours): say who each part is for.
- Figures without a source read as made up: fewer and real beats many.

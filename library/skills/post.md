# Post

Something to read: an article, a story, news, a notice. A visitor wants to read it comfortably, know when it was written and by whom, and find more to read.

## What the page needs
- A title that says what it is about, the date, the author when there is one, and a short lead.
- The text itself, in the request's words: short paragraphs, headings for a long text, pictures with captions where they help.
- For a notice: what changes, for whom, from when, and what to do, before any background.
- More to read: other posts of the site, or the page it belongs to.

## What only the person can tell you
The text, the date, the author, the facts and quotes. When the request gives the text, keep it: correct obvious typos only, and never add facts, quotes or figures. When it gives only a subject, write a draft and say in your answer that it is a draft to check.

## Links
- Other posts: the site's pages (/<page>/); pages it doesn't have yet at the address they will have, named in your answer.
- Sources and things the text mentions on other websites: their full address (https://…).
- Share links only when the request asks for them.

## Watch for
- Body text no wider than about 70 characters, and a size that reads comfortably on a phone.
- A post is read, not used: no buttons competing with the text.

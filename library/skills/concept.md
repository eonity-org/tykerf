# Concept

A product, an idea or a project people should try or back: an app, a service, a book, a campaign. A visitor wants to know what it is, what it does for them, whether to trust it, and the next step.

## What the page needs
- What it is and who it is for, in one sentence, with the main action beside it (try it, sign up, back it, buy it).
- What it does: three to six things, each as what the visitor gets, not as a feature list.
- How it works: the steps, when it isn't obvious.
- A closer look: a picture or a drawing of the product in use.
- Prices, only when the request gives them: what each plan includes, and what happens after a free period.
- The questions people ask before they decide, and the next step again at the end.

## What only the person can tell you
The name, the prices and plans, the address of the app or the shop, customers, figures, quotes from users, the company behind it. Use what the request gives. A price or a plan you don't know is a plain example listed at the end of your answer to confirm.

## Links
- Sign up, log in, the app, a store: the address the person gives (https://…), or a page the site doesn't have yet at the address it will have (/signup/), named in your answer.
- Terms and a privacy policy, when people pay or give their data: /terms/ and /privacy/ if the site doesn't have them yet, named in your answer.
- Every call to action of the page goes to the same next step.

## Watch for
- One main action, repeated: not three different buttons.
- A project or a campaign is not a product: back it, join it or read more, rather than pricing.

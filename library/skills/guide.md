# Guide

Something to follow or look up: a how-to, a recipe, a help page, documentation. A visitor wants to get something done: see quickly whether it is the right guide, then follow it step by step, often with their hands busy.

## What the page needs
- What it is for, who it is for, and what they need before they start (ingredients, tools, a version, an account).
- The steps, numbered, one action each, in the order they happen; times and quantities where they matter.
- The details to look up: options, settings, variations, with headings that name them.
- The questions and problems people meet, each with its answer.

## What only the person can tell you
Quantities, times, temperatures, commands, versions, settings, the product's real names. Use what the request gives. Never invent a command, an option or a quantity that could make someone fail: when one is missing, write a plain example and list it at the end of your answer to check.

## Links
- Within the page: each part a section with an id, linked from the top (#steps).
- Other guides of the site: /<page>/; ones it doesn't have yet at the address they will have, named in your answer.
- Downloads, source code, the product's site: their full address (https://…), only the real ones.

## Watch for
- Code and commands in code blocks people can copy, never in pictures.
- A recipe reads from the kitchen: ingredients beside or before the steps, large enough text.

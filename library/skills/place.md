# Place

A place people visit: a shop, a bakery, a restaurant, a studio, a venue. A visitor wants to know what it is, whether it is for them, when it is open and how to get there, usually on a phone, often on the way.

## What the page needs
- What the place is, in one line, with a real picture of it or of what it offers.
- What it offers: a few things, said the way the place says them (the breads, the dishes, the treatments), prices only when the request gives them.
- When it is open and where: hours by day, the address, a link to a map, how to get there if it is hard to find.
- How to reach it: the phone (as a tel: link, so a phone can call it), an email or a booking link.
- On a phone, the hours, the address and the phone come before anything long.

## What only the person can tell you
The hours, the address, the phone, the prices, the dishes or products, the booking service. Use what the request gives. When it doesn't give one of them, don't present a made-up value as real: write a plain example, and list it at the end of your answer as something to confirm ("Hours: I wrote Monday to Saturday, 8 to 14; tell me yours").

## Links
- A map: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=<the address>, or the map link the person gives.
- A phone: tel:+<country><number>; an email: mailto:.
- A booking or delivery service, a social profile: its full address (https://…). Without the real one, leave the link out and ask for it.

## Watch for
- A contact form for a place that takes calls is one step too many: a phone and an address may be enough.

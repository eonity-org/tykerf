# Person

One person: a CV, a freelancer, an artist, a professional. A visitor wants to know who they are, whether they can do what the visitor needs, see proof of it, and reach them.

## What the page needs
- Who they are and what they do, in their own voice (first person, unless the request says otherwise), with their photo or their work.
- What they have done: their path (jobs, studies, shows) newest first, with dates; for a freelancer, the services or the kinds of work they take.
- The work itself, for whoever shows it: pictures, projects, publications, each with a line saying what it is.
- How to reach them: an email, a phone or a form, and where else they are (LinkedIn, a portfolio site, Instagram).

## What only the person can tell you
Their name, dates, employers, schools, clients, prizes, their email and phone, their profiles elsewhere. Use what the request gives. Never invent a job, a degree, a client or a prize: leave out what you don't know, and list what the page would need at the end of your answer. A contact detail you can't know is written as a plain example and listed to replace.

## Links
- Profiles elsewhere (LinkedIn, Instagram, Behance, GitHub): their full address (https://…), and only with the person's real one; without it, leave the link out and ask for it. Never as a page of this site (/instagram/).
- An email: mailto:; a phone: tel:.
- A CV to download: a file the person gives (add_file); without one, leave it out.

## Watch for
- A CV is read quickly: short lines, dates easy to find, no long paragraphs.
- A portfolio is about the work: let the pictures lead, and keep the words short.
